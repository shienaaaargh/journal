## 🚕 Slowly Seeing the Bigger Picture

Starting Saturday, I spent a lot of time trying to understand our **NYC Mobility project**, especially yung data model and kung paano namin sasagutin yung business questions.

At first, nalilito ako kung bakit kailangan namin ng different fact tables, dimensions, keys, and even a fact constellation. Parang iniisip ko, *“Bakit yung ibang group isang fact table lang, tapos kami ang dami?”* 😭

Pero habang ine-explain at kinukumpara namin yung schemas, mas nagkaroon ako ng idea na hindi naman paramihan or pakontian ng tables ang goal. Ang importante pala is kung ano yung **grain ng data**, ano yung business question, at paano connected yung facts and dimensions.

Dito ko rin mas na-realize na hindi enough na gumagana lang yung query. Kailangan maintindihan ko kung **bakit ganito yung design** at kung tama ba siya for the problem we are trying to solve.

## 🧪 Incremental Loading: From “Ano Ito?” to Actual Evidence

One of my biggest tasks this week was **Issue #3 — incremental loading verification**.

Honestly, noong una hindi ko talaga fully naiintindihan kung ano yung purpose niya. Ang nasa isip ko lang was March → April → May and kailangan mag-pass yung tests.

Eventually, nag-click sa akin na ang chine-check pala namin is kapag nagdagdag kami ng bagong buwan ng data, **hindi dapat masira, mapalitan, or ma-duplicate yung mga naunang data**.

I worked on the March baseline, then checked that April preserved March, and May preserved both March and April. I also verified the related idempotency tests.

Seeing:

**7/7 incremental loading tests passed**  
and  
**7/7 idempotency tests passed**

felt really satisfying. ✨

Pero dito ko rin natutunan na hindi porket green yung tests, ibig sabihin pwede na agad sabihin na na-prove na namin everything.

Our tests use **synthetic snapshots**, so they prove the transformation behavior, but they are not automatically proof that an actual persisted production table was incrementally written in Databricks.

That distinction was small at first, pero ngayon naiintindihan ko na kung bakit sobrang importante ng wording sa engineering documentation.

## 📝 Documentation Is Part of the Engineering

Akala ko dati documentation is parang last step lang after matapos yung code.

This week proved me wrong. 😂

While working on the incremental loading evidence and runbook, nagkaroon pa ako ng problems sa Markdown formatting, duplicated content, code blocks, staged files, commits, and PR updates.

Nakakainis minsan kasi feeling ko, *“Test lang naman dapat ginagawa ko, bakit pati Markdown pinoproblema ko?”* 😭

Pero na-realize ko rin na if someone else cannot understand **what I tested, how I tested it, and what the result actually proves**, kulang pa rin yung work.

So documentation is not just explanation after engineering.

**Part siya ng engineering.**

## 🔍 Being a Reviewer Is Harder Than I Expected

Another new experience for me was reviewing someone else’s work for **Issue #7 and PR #32**.

Dito ako medyo nalito kasi green naman yung tests and maraming acceptance criteria ang completed, pero meron pang requirement na kailangan makita yung **controlled red → green CI evidence**.

At first, iniisip ko:

*“Hindi ba enough na pinakita na nag-fail locally yung test?”*

Pero ngayon mas clear na sa akin yung difference.

A local failed pytest proves na kayang mahuli ng test yung error.

Pero yung Issue #7 specifically wants proof na:

**broken code → GitHub Actions becomes RED → code is fixed → GitHub Actions becomes GREEN.**

And as reviewer, kailangan ko rin ma-confirm na yung intentionally broken commit **hindi napunta sa main or production**.

This made me realize na reviewing a PR is not just checking kung maganda yung code.

Kailangan pala bumalik ka sa **acceptance criteria** and ask:

> “May evidence ba talaga para sabihin nating completed ito?”

## 🧩 “Para Saan Ito?” Became My Most Important Question

One pattern I noticed about myself this week is lagi akong nagtatanong ng:

**“Para saan ito?”**

Incremental loading — para saan?

Workflow recovery — para saan?

CI — para saan?

Semantic versioning — para saan?

Bakit connected yung issues?

Bakit may dependencies?

Bakit hindi pwedeng i-merge kahit green yung tests?

Before, minsan gusto ko lang malaman yung exact command na kailangan i-run para matapos yung task.

Ngayon, mas gusto ko nang maintindihan muna yung **purpose ng task sa buong system**.

Semantic versioning was another example. Kahit ilang explanation, hindi agad nag-click sa akin yung MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH. Pero nung kinonekta siya sa actual project changes namin—like adding backward-compatible functionality versus changing a schema that could break existing queries—mas nagkaroon siya ng meaning.

I still have a lot to learn, pero I think this is progress.

Hindi ko na lang tinatanong:

> “Ano yung gagawin ko?”

Mas madalas ko nang tinatanong:

> **“Bakit natin ito ginagawa?”**

## ⚙️ Understanding the Pipeline as One System

Another thing na mas nagiging clear sa akin is hindi pala independent yung GitHub issues namin.

Configuration affects incremental loading.

Incremental loading and idempotency affect safe reruns.

Safe reruns matter kapag may workflow failure.

Workflow recovery depends on orchestration.

Data-quality checks need to fit into that workflow.

Then CI protects the code before those changes are merged.

Before, parang nakikita ko sila as separate assignments assigned to different people.

Ngayon, slowly nakikita ko na sila as parts of **one production data pipeline**.

Hindi pa perfect yung understanding ko, especially sa orchestration and recovery behavior, pero at least ngayon may picture na ako kung saan sila pumapasok.
